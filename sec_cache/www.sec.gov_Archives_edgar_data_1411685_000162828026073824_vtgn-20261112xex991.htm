Vistagen Reports Fiscal Year 2027 Second Quarter Financial Results and Provides Corporate Update  

Advancing potential to execute Phase 3 development plan for fasedienol in social anxiety disorder following constructive FDA feedback, with disciplined capital management and strategic flexibility

SOUTH SAN FRANCISCO, Calif. (BUSINESS WIRE) — November 13, 2026 — Vistagen (Nasdaq: VTGN), a late clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company pioneering neuroscience with nose-to-brain neurocircuitry to develop and commercialize a new class of intranasal product candidates called pherines, today reported financial results for its fiscal year 2027 second quarter ended September 30, 2026, and provided a corporate update.

“Following our recent productive meeting with the FDA and our evaluation of the extensive body of efficacy, safety, and patient experience data generated throughout Phase 2 and Phase 3 development of fasedienol, we believe we have clarity on the potential regulatory pathways forward for fasedienol in social anxiety disorder” said Shawn Singh, President and Chief Executive Officer of Vistagen. “Recent alignment of our operating structure and capital resources with our late-stage development opportunities in both social anxiety disorder and menopausal hot flashes, position us to execute our plan to advance these programs while maintaining flexibility to create long-term shareholder value.”

Corporate and Program Highlights

Phase 3 Development of Fasedienol in Social Anxiety Disorder

•FDA Type C Meeting Feedback: The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provided constructive feedback regarding Vistagen’s potential Phase 3 study of fasedienol in adults with social anxiety disorder (SAD), including recommendations regarding use of the Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS) as the primary endpoint and interim assessments to assess whether acute effects of fasedienol could lead to longer-term overall functional improvement in patients as measured by the LSAS over time, consistent with the unique proposed mechanism of action and hypothesized treatment model for fasedienol. Feedback from the recent meeting also included FDA’s recognition of fasedienol’s reported safety profile and potential to benefit a broad population of patients with SAD. Based on the FDA’s feedback, Vistagen plans to focus future Phase 3 clinical development of fasedienol on the overall treatment of SAD (not only acute treatment) for the general SAD population.

•PALISADE-4 Phase 3 Study OLE: Announced preliminary positive data from the open-label extension (OLE) portion of PALISADE-4, the Company’s Phase 3 study of fasedienol for the acute treatment of social anxiety disorder. Clinically relevant improvement in social anxiety over time was observed on both clinician-administered and patient-reported scales over four months, as measured by the LSAS. The Company believes placebo-controlled Phase 2 data and data from the OLE portion of its PALISADE Phase 3 trials conducted to date in everyday life settings have shown that acute treatment with fasedienol, administered as-needed at the patient’s discretion up to six times a day, has the potential to provide therapeutic benefit in anxiety-provoking situations over time.

Corporate Updates

•New Board Member: Appointed Douglas J. Williamson, MD to its Board of Directors, bringing decades of neuroscience drug development and commercialization experience from Acadia Pharmaceuticals, Eli Lilly, Lundbeck, Avadel Pharmaceuticals, and QurAlis.

•Cost Management Initiatives: Implemented targeted cost-management initiatives designed to prioritize capital allocation across prioritized clinical-stage programs and- extend operating runway.

•Psych Congress Presentation: Presented Phase 3 data demonstrating a positive efficacy signal for fasedienol in participants with very severe social anxiety disorder at Psych Congress 2026.



Financial Results for Fiscal Year 2027 Second Quarter Ended September 30, 2026

Research and development (R&D) expense: R&D expense was $6.6 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, as compared to $15.9 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The decrease was primarily due to lower clinical development expenses following the completion of the open label extension portions of the Company’s PALISADE-3 and PALISADE-4 Phase 3 studies and the randomized and open label extension portions of its exploratory Phase 2 repeat dose study as well as reduced headcount-related expenses.

General and administrative (G&A) expense: G&A expense was $4.5 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, as compared to $4.4 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The increase was primarily due to higher consulting and professional fees, driven by higher legal fees, partially offset by lower compensation and related costs associated with reduced headcount-related expenses and lower stock-based compensation expense.

Net loss: Net loss was $10.7 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, as compared to $19.4 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025.

Cash position: Cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities were $20.0 million as of September 30, 2026. Based on current operating plans, Vistagen believes its cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities will be sufficient to fund operations into 2027 but are not expected to be sufficient to extend the Company’s cash runway for more than a year from now.

About Vistagen

Vistagen (Nasdaq: VTGN) is a late clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company leveraging a deep understanding of nose-to-brain neurocircuitry to develop and commercialize a new class of rapid-onset neurocircuitry-focused intranasal product candidates called pherines. Vistagen’s pherine product candidates are designed to achieve therapeutic benefits without requiring absorption into the blood or uptake into the brain, giving them the potential to be a safer alternative to other pharmacological options if successfully developed and approved. Vistagen’s pherine pipeline currently consists of five investigational product candidates focused on improving the current standard of care for multiple highly prevalent indications, including social anxiety disorder, major depressive disorder, and vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes) due to menopause.
