Astera Labs Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results

•Record quarterly revenue of $584.9 million, up 49% QoQ and up 154% year-over-year
•Scorpio™ fabric switches became the company's largest product family, led by volume production of the Scorpio X-Series 320-lane fabric switch
•Portfolio expanded across memory, optical and copper signal conditioning, and rack-scale AI platforms with Leo 2 and Leo X-Series, Aries 7 and Orion

SAN JOSE, CA, U.S. – November 3, 2026 – Astera Labs, Inc. (Nasdaq: ALAB), a leader in semiconductor-based connectivity solutions for rack-scale AI infrastructure, today announced financial results for the third quarter of fiscal year 2026, ended September 30, 2026.

“Astera Labs delivered outstanding Q3 2026 results, with record quarterly revenue of $584.9 million, up 49% sequentially and up 154% year-over-year, with broad-based growth led by Scorpio, which ramped to high-volume production and became our largest product family by revenue,” said Jitendra Mohan, Astera Labs’ Chief Executive Officer. “Design activity accelerated with wins across a broad set of programs and applications at multiple customers. We expanded our portfolio with the introduction of Aries 7 for optical and copper signal conditioning, Leo 2 and Leo X-Series for AI and general-purpose memory expansion, and Orion rack-scale reference architecture. The expansion of our intelligent connectivity platform and new design wins position us to strongly grow our dollar content per accelerator and further increase our total market opportunity.”

Q3 2026 and Recent Business Highlights

•Announced Aries 7, the industry’s first and broadest PCIe® 7 signal conditioning portfolio, spanning Smart Retimers, Smart Redrivers, Smart Cable Modules, Smart Optical Drivers, and Smart Optical Transimpedance Amplifiers (TIAs). The expanded portfolio extends reach from centimeters of PCB trace to 50+ meters over optical cables spanning multiple racks and includes optical connectivity solutions purpose-built for near-packaged optics (NPO) and linear pluggable optics (LPO) applications. Aries 7 Smart Retimers and Smart Redrivers support the OCP Signal Conditioner Standard Footprint, allowing customers to select different signal conditioners without redesigning the board. Aries 7 products are expected to begin sampling in Q4 2026 to select hyperscale, OEM, and NPO customers.
•Unveiled Orion, a compute-agnostic rack-scale reference architecture that allows customers to choose their own GPU and CPU. Orion comprises six production-grade reference designs—spanning rack, compute tray, fabric tray, memory tray, link board, and compute board—and its high-radix 320-lane PCIe switch fabric is designed to scale from 8 to 160 accelerators per pod while helping the same accelerator extract more tokens per second across AI inference workloads. All designs are integrated with COSMOS Rack Manager and COSMOS Fabric Manager orchestration software for management, fleet-wide telemetry and diagnostics. Orion is designed primarily for neocloud and enterprise customers building AI inference capacity, and rack reference and system designs are on track for availability through Astera Labs’ OEM and ODM partners beginning in Q1 2027.
•Expanded the Leo Smart Memory Controller™ family with three new memory connectivity solutions—Leo X-Series and the Leo 2 E-Series and P-Series—extending the portfolio across direct fabric-attached GPU memory, CPU-attached expansion, and pooled memory architectures. Paired with Scorpio X-Series Fabric Switches, Leo X-Series enables fabric-attached memory for long-context, KV-cache-intensive workloads, improving inference performance with up to 62% faster time-to-first-token and up to 22% more tokens per second (as measured by the company’s internal testing). The new Leo 2 E-Series and P-Series support CPU-attached DDR4 and DDR5 memory expansion with PCIe® 6 and CXL® 3.2 support, and double memory bandwidth and capacity versus the previous generation.
•Showcased the expanded Aries and Orion families at the 2026 Open Compute Project (OCP) Global Summit in San Jose, including a powered Orion rack featuring interchangeable, pre-validated compute trays with different CPU and accelerator configurations and live demonstrations of the Scorpio X-Series 320-lane Smart Fabric Switch with Hypercast™ and In-Network Compute, the Leo X-Series Smart Memory Controller, and PCIe 7 copper and optical signal conditioning.

Discussion of Non-GAAP Financial Measures
We use certain non-GAAP financial measures, including those concerning our financial outlook, to supplement the performance measures in our consolidated financial statements, which are presented in accordance with GAAP. A reconciliation of these non-GAAP measures to the closest GAAP measures can be found later in this release. The timing and impact of any adjustments to arrive at the corresponding GAAP financial measures concerning our financial outlook are inherently dependent on future events that are typically uncertain or that may be outside of our control. These non-GAAP financial measures include non-GAAP gross profit, non-GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP operating expenses, non-GAAP operating income, non-GAAP operating margin, non-GAAP tax rate, non-GAAP net income, and non-GAAP diluted net income per share. We use these non-GAAP financial measures for financial and operational decision-making and as a means to assist us in evaluating period-to-period comparisons. By excluding certain items that may not be indicative of our recurring core operating results, we believe that non-GAAP gross profit, non-GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP operating expenses, non-GAAP operating income, non-GAAP operating margin, non-GAAP tax rate, non-GAAP net income, and non-GAAP diluted net income per share provide meaningful supplemental information regarding our performance. Accordingly, we believe these non-GAAP financial measures are useful to investors and others because they allow for additional information with respect to financial measures used by management in its financial and operational decision-making and they may be used by our institutional investors and the analyst community to help them analyze the health of our business. However, there are a number of limitations related to the use of non-GAAP financial measures, and these non-GAAP measures should be considered in addition to, not as a substitute for or in isolation from, our financial results prepared in accordance with GAAP. Other companies, including companies in our industry, may calculate these non-GAAP financial measures differently or not at all, which reduces their usefulness as comparative measures.

We adjust the following items from one or more of our non-GAAP financial measures:

Stock-based compensation expense
We exclude non-cash stock-based compensation expense from certain of our non-GAAP financial measures because we believe that excluding this item provides meaningful supplemental information regarding operational performance. In particular, companies calculate non-cash stock-based compensation expense using a variety of valuation methodologies and subjective assumptions. Moreover, stock-based compensation expense is a non-cash charge that can vary significantly from period to period for reasons that are unrelated to our core operating performance, and therefore excluding this item provides investors and other users of our financial information with information that allows meaningful comparisons of our business performance across periods.



Acquisition-related costs
We exclude acquisition-related costs incurred in connection with our acquisitions, which we generally would not have otherwise incurred in the periods presented as part of our continuing operations. Acquisition-related costs include certain incremental expenses incurred to effect a business combination such as third-party costs: advisory, legal, accounting, valuation, and other professional fees. We believe that providing the non-GAAP measures excluding these costs assists our investors because such costs are not reflective of our ongoing operating results.

Other
We exclude other adjustments, including non-cash fair value adjustments related to equity investments without readily determinable fair values. These investments are measured at cost and adjusted for observable price changes or impairment on a nonrecurring basis only upon the occurrence of certain events. Accordingly, these adjustments are not indicative of our core operating performance.

Income tax effect
This represents the impact of the non-GAAP adjustments on an after-tax basis and one-off discrete tax adjustments that are unrelated to our core operating performance in connection with the presentation of non-GAAP net income and non-GAAP diluted net income per share. This approach is designed to enhance investors’ ability to understand the impact of our non-GAAP tax expense on our current operations, provide improved modeling accuracy, and substantially reduce fluctuations caused by GAAP to non-GAAP adjustments.

About Astera Labs
Astera Labs (Nasdaq: ALAB) provides rack-scale AI infrastructure through purpose-built connectivity solutions. By collaborating with hyperscalers and ecosystem partners, Astera Labs enables organizations to unlock the full potential of modern AI. Astera Labs' Intelligent Connectivity Platform integrates CXL®, Ethernet, NVLink Fusion, PCIe®, and UALink™ semiconductor-based technologies with the company's COSMOS software suite to unify diverse components into cohesive, flexible systems that deliver end-to-end scale-up and scale-out connectivity. The company's custom connectivity solutions business complements its standards-based portfolio, enabling customers to deploy tailored architectures to meet their unique infrastructure requirements.
