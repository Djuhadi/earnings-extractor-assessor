AMC Entertainment Holdings, Inc. Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results, Setting New All-Time Records for Quarterly Revenue

and Adjusted EBITDA

​

●Revenue and Adjusted EBITDA surpass the previous all-time highs established in Q2 2026.
●This was AMC’s second consecutive record-setting quarter.
●Prior to 2026, AMC’s all-time quarterly revenue record was set in Q2 2019, and its all-time quarterly Adjusted EBITDA record was set in Q4 2017

​

​

LEAWOOD, KANSAS - (November 4, 2026) -- AMC Entertainment Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: AMC) (“AMC” or “the Company”), exceeded Wall Street expectations on several key metrics in its reporting today of results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

​

Third Quarter 2026 Summary Results Compared to the Third Quarter of 2025:

●Total revenues were $1,711.1 million compared to $1,300.2 million, up 31.6%.
●Net earnings (loss) was $109.3 million compared to $(298.2) million, a $407.5 million improvement.
●Adjusted net earnings (loss) was $116.9 million compared to $(110.0) million, a $226.9 million improvement.
●Adjusted EBITDA was $347.5 million compared to $122.5 million, up 183.7%.
●Net cash provided by (used in) operating activities was $176.6 million compared to $(14.9) million, a $191.5 million improvement.
●Cash and cash equivalents at September 30, 2026 were $751.9 million, excluding restricted cash of $39.6 million, compared to $365.8 million, up 105.5%.

Nine months ended September 30, 2026, Summary Results Compared to Nine months ended September 30, 2025:

●Total revenues were $4,353.2 million compared to $3,560.6 million, up 22.3%.
●Net (loss) was $(19.2) million compared to $(505.0) million, a $485.8 million improvement.
●Adjusted net earnings (loss) was $26.9 million compared to $(360.5) million, a $387.4 million improvement.
●Adjusted EBITDA was $707.2 million compared to $254.3 million, up 178.1%.
●Net cash provided by (used in) operating activities was $283.5 million compared to $(246.5) million, a $530.0 million improvement.

​

Commenting on AMC’s third quarter and year-to-date 2026 results, AMC Chairman and CEO Adam Aron said, “Sometimes short but sweet is the best way to summarize performance. Notably, in the third quarter of 2026, AMC Entertainment set all-time records for achieving the highest quarterly revenue ($1,711.1 million) and highest quarterly Adjusted EBITDA ($347.5 million) for any quarter in our company’s 106-year history. It is the second quarter in a row that AMC set all-time records for revenue and Adjusted EBITDA. On revenue, first Q2 of 2026 and now again Q3 of 2026 surpassed AMC’s prior all-time record set in Q2 of 2019. On Adjusted EBITDA, first Q2 of 2026 and now again Q3 of 2026 surpassed AMC’s prior all-time record set in Q4 of 2017.”

Aron continued, “AMC turned audience enthusiasm into exceptional third quarter financial results thanks to our industry-leading loyalty programs along with the vast and growing number of AMC’s premium large format and extra large format auditoriums, combined with disciplined cost management, superb execution and the operating leverage inherent in our business. Our success was driven by a resurgent box office, market share gains in both domestic and international markets, and the achievement of all-time record revenue per patron. Accordingly, our Q3 revenues increased by 31.6% compared to the third quarter of last year. Our Q3 Adjusted EBITDA grew by 183.7%, nearly triple the result of last year’s third quarter, while our Adjusted EBITDA margin reached a record of 20.3%, more than double the 9.4% recorded in last year’s third quarter. Q3 Free Cash Flow was $109.3 million, an improvement of more than $190 million year over year, bringing year-to-date Free Cash Flow to $124.7 million. This gives us confidence to expect that AMC will achieve the much-welcomed milestone of positive annual Free Cash Flow for the full year 2026.”

Aron added, “Alongside these record-setting operating results, we recently took another extraordinarily important step forward in strengthening AMC’s financial foundation. On October 5, we completed a $3.97 billion refinancing covering approximately 97% of our debt, extending maturities to late 2031 and 2033, simplifying our capital structure, and reducing our cost of capital. This transaction builds on years of bold and creative capital markets actions taken. Indeed, the major credit rating agencies have recognized the significant strengthening of AMC’s credit profile and meaningfully upgraded our credit rating. AMC now moves forward from a position of significantly increased balance sheet strength.”

Aron highlighted, “And the excitement continues with a surging fourth quarter domestic box office. The October domestic box office was up approximately 35% above the same month last year. And some of the year’s most anticipated films are still to come in November and December, including among other titles I PLAY ROCKY, WILD HORSE NINE, EBENEZER, THE FURTHER MISADVENTURES OF CLIFF BOOTH, THE HUNGER GAMES: SUNRISE ON THE REAPING, DUNE: PART THREE, AVENGERS: DOOMSDAY, MR. IRRELEVANT and JUMANJI: OPEN WORLD. It is an impressive and promising film slate that is a fitting close to a wonderful year for moviegoers. We expect the remaining film slate to propel both the domestic and global box office to their highest full-year levels in seven years. We could not be more enthusiastic about AMC’s prospects as this year draws to a triumphant close.”

Aron concluded, "In recent years, where other key players in our industry collapsed, at AMC we repeatedly instead have talked about recovery. Today, we can finally say that AMC has recovered. To our studio partners, lenders, business partners, employees and our millions of passionate shareholders, thank you for standing with us as we traveled through a long, and at times brutal, road. Mere survival was never the goal. Our goal ultimately was to thrive, and that is exactly and precisely where our focus now lies. We are highly energized by the tremendous opportunities ahead.”

Cash, Balance Sheet, and Capital Markets Activity

Cash at September 30, 2026 was $751.9 million, excluding restricted cash of $39.6 million.

Third Quarter 2026

On July 24, 2026, AMC fully redeemed $125.5 million aggregate principal amount of the 6.125% Senior Subordinated Notes due 2027.

Fourth Quarter 2026

On October 5, 2026, AMC successfully completed the refinancing of approximately 97% of its existing debt, materially extending the Company’s debt maturities until 2031 and 2033, simplifying its capital structure and reducing the average interest rate on its debt.

●As part of the refinancing, AMC:
oIssued $2.0 billion aggregate principal amount of 8.875% first lien notes due 2031;
oEntered into a new $850 million aggregate principal amount first lien term loan facility, and;
oEntered into a new $1.12 billion aggregate principal amount second lien term loan facility.
●Net proceeds have been used to satisfy and discharge the indenture governing AMC’s 7.500% senior secured notes due 2029, redeem all of Muvico’s senior secured notes due 2029, fully repay AMC’s and Odeon Finco’s
existing term loans, and cover related transaction costs.

About AMC Entertainment Holdings, Inc.

AMC is the largest movie exhibition company in the United States, the largest in Europe and the largest throughout the world with approximately 840 theatres and 9,500 screens across the globe. AMC has propelled innovation in the exhibition industry by: deploying its signature power-recliner seats; delivering enhanced food and beverage choices; generating greater guest engagement through its loyalty and subscription programs, website, and mobile apps; offering premium large format experiences and playing a wide variety of content including the latest Hollywood releases and independent programming. For more information, visit www.amctheatres.com/.

1)We present Adjusted EBITDA as a supplemental measure of our performance. We define Adjusted EBITDA as net earnings (loss) plus (i) income tax provision (benefit), (ii) interest expense and (iii) depreciation and amortization, as further adjusted to eliminate the impact of certain items that we do not consider indicative of our ongoing operating performance and to include attributable EBITDA from equity investments in theatre operations in International markets. These further adjustments are itemized above. You are encouraged to evaluate these adjustments and the reasons we consider them appropriate for supplemental analysis. In evaluating Adjusted EBITDA, you should be aware that in the future we may incur expenses that are the same as or similar to some of the adjustments in this presentation. Our presentation of Adjusted EBITDA should not be construed as an inference that our future results will be unaffected by unusual or non-recurring items. Adjusted EBITDA is a non-U.S. GAAP financial measure commonly used in our industry and should not be construed as an alternative to net earnings (loss) as an indicator of operating performance (as determined in accordance with U.S. GAAP). Adjusted EBITDA may not be comparable to similarly titled measures reported by other companies. We have included Adjusted EBITDA because we believe it provides management and investors with additional information to measure our performance and estimate our value. Our definition of Adjusted EBITDA and adjustments made to net earnings (loss) to calculate it are broadly consistent with how Adjusted EBITDA is defined and calculated in our debt indentures.

​

During the first quarter of 2026, we changed our definition of Adjusted EBITDA to adjust for net periodic pension cost. Net periodic pension cost is a recurring expense that includes several components such as service cost, interest cost, expected return on plan assets, amortization of prior service cost, and amortization of actuarial gains/losses. Additionally, we also include infrequent gains and losses from benefit curtailments and settlements of pension obligations in net periodic pension cost. We no longer believe that net periodic pension cost should be included in Adjusted EBITDA as the pension plans are frozen, service cost is zero, and the remaining components are not indicative of ongoing operating performance as they are not driven by current operating decisions and largely depend on actuarial assumptions. While not the basis for this change, the revised definition further aligns our definition of Adjusted EBITDA with the definition used in our debt agreements. The adjustment for net periodic pension cost is included in the caption titled “other expense” in the condensed consolidated statement of operations and in the reconciliation of net earnings (loss) to Adjusted EBITDA. All comparative period information for Adjusted EBITDA has been re-cast to conform with the current definition. The impact of this change on previously reported Adjusted EBITDA for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2025 was an improvement of $0.3 million and $0.9 million, respectively.

​

Adjusted EBITDA has important limitations as an analytical tool, and you should not consider it in isolation, or as a substitute for analysis of our results as reported under U.S. GAAP. For example, Adjusted EBITDA:

​

●does not reflect our capital expenditures, future requirements for capital expenditures or contractual commitments;
●does not reflect changes in, or cash requirements for, our working capital needs;
●does not reflect the significant interest expenses, or the cash requirements necessary to service interest or principal payments, on our debt;
●excludes income tax payments that represent a reduction in cash available to us; and
●does not reflect any cash requirements for the assets being depreciated and amortized that may have to be replaced in the future.

1)We present “contribution margin” and “contribution margin per patron” as supplemental measures of our performance. We define “contribution margin” as Revenue less both Film Exhibition Costs and Food and Beverage Costs. These costs are directly variable with attendance. Contribution margin per patron is the total contribution margin divided by the number of customers served. The “contribution margin per patron” represents the incremental dollars earned or (lost) per customer gained or (lost). We believe contribution margin and contribution margin per patron are key performance measures that provide investors with supplemental information regarding (i) the impact on our profitability of differing attendance levels, after deducting the direct variable costs associated with those attendance levels, but before recognizing the impact of fixed operating costs and expenses that do not vary directly with attendance and (ii) our ability to cover fixed costs that do not vary directly with attendance. We believe this is particularly important information given the significant variability in attendance levels in our business and our industry.

“Contribution margin” has important limitations as an analytical tool and should be evaluated only in conjunction with our results as reported under U.S. GAAP and other performance measures such as Adjusted EBITDA. Our definition of “contribution margin” as set forth in the reconciliation above, is the equivalent of GAAP gross profit (loss) after adding back Operating expense, excluding depreciation and amortization expense; Rent, and Depreciation and amortization expense, which in each case are otherwise included in cost of revenue. As a result, while “contribution margin” is designed to focus on the impact of directly variable costs, it excludes normal, recurring, operating expenses that do not vary directly with attendance, but which nevertheless directly impact our profitability.

We present adjusted net earnings (loss) for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share and adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share as supplemental measures of our performance. We have included these measures because we believe they provide management and investors with additional information that is helpful when evaluating our underlying performance and comparing our results on a year-over-year normalized basis. Adjusted net earnings (loss) for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share eliminates the impact of certain items that we do not consider indicative of our underlying operating performance. These adjustments are itemized above.

Adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share is adjusted net earnings (loss) for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share divided by weighted average shares for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share. Weighted average shares for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share include common stock equivalents for restricted stock units (“RSUs”), performance stock units (“PSUs”), and shares issuable upon exchange of our exchangeable notes. The dilutive effect of the conversion feature of the Existing Exchangeable Notes was anti-dilutive for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026. No incremental shares for RSUs and PSUs were added in determining weighted average shares for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share for the three months ended September 30, 2026 as their dilutive effect had already been captured in the weighted average shares for diluted earnings (loss) per share. The dilutive effect of the New Exchangeable Notes was anti-dilutive for the period the New Exchangeable Notes were outstanding during the nine months ended September 30, 2026. The dilutive effect of RSUs, PSUs, and exchangeable notes were excluded from the three months ended September 30, 2025 because an adjusted net loss exists. The dilutive effect of RSUs, PSUs, and exchangeable notes were excluded from the nine months ended September 30, 2025 because an adjusted net loss exists.

You are encouraged to evaluate the adjustments itemized above and the reasons we consider them appropriate for supplemental analysis. In evaluating adjusted net earnings (loss) for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share and adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share, you should be aware that in the future we may incur expenses that are the same as or similar to some of the adjustments in this presentation. Our presentation of adjusted net earnings (loss) for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share and adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share should not be construed as an inference that our future results will be unaffected by unusual or non-recurring items. Adjusted net earnings (loss) for adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share and adjusted diluted earnings (loss) per share are non-U.S. GAAP financial measures and should not be construed as alternatives to net earnings (loss) and diluted earnings (loss) per share as indicators of operating performance (as determined in accordance with U.S. GAAP).
