HOWARD HUGHES HOLDINGS INC. REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
Vantage transition to HHH in full effect; Howard Hughes Communities® advances asset-light strategy for real estate development and asset management platform

THE WOODLANDS, Texas, November 5, 2026 – Howard Hughes Holdings Inc. (NYSE: HHH) (the “Company,” “HHH,” “Howard Hughes,” or “we”) today reported third quarter 2026 results as highlighted in the following paragraphs.

Third Quarter 2026 Highlights:

–Net loss attributable to common stockholders was $1.1 million for the quarter, compared to net income of $119.5 million in the prior-year period.
–Vantage posts solid results during first full quarter in Howard Hughes. Vantage contributed $315.8 million of net earned insurance premiums and $76.6 million of income before income taxes.
–Howard Hughes Communities (HHC) delivered Master Planned Communities (MPC) EBT of $89.5 million and Total Operating Assets Net Operating Income (NOI) of $73.0 million in the quarter. Further details and prior-year comparisons are presented in Financial Highlights below.

Financial Highlights

Insurance and Reinsurance

–Net earned insurance and reinsurance premiums were $315.8 million for the third quarter 2026.
–Reported underwriting loss was $5.7 million, with a combined ratio of 101.8%, comprising a loss ratio of 63.5% and an expense ratio of 38.3%. These results include the impact of purchase accounting. Without these adjustments, the underlying results represent underwriting income of $16.1 million, with a combined ratio of 94.9%.
–Losses included no catastrophe losses, and $4.0 million of favorable prior-year development, but were impacted by a single current large loss in a specialty line and purchase accounting adjustments resulting in a loss ratio for the quarter of 63.5%.
–The investment portfolio delivered net insurance investment income of $22.7 million and net investment gains of $50.9 million, representing the transition of the portfolio to one based on common equities and U.S. treasuries.
–The AdVantage insurance-linked securities (ILS) structure contributed $13.0 million in net fee income, resulting from the continued positive outcomes for recent property catastrophe business written in this structure.
–Income before income taxes was $76.6 million, including the impact of purchase accounting.

Real Estate

–MPC EBT of $89.5 million in the third quarter was down $115.5 million from $205.0 million in the prior-year period primarily due to bulk sale of 231 acres in Summerlin that occurred during the third quarter of 2025, as well as a $19.4 million loss associated with the consolidation of the Floreo joint venture.
–Pricing remained strong during the first nine months of 2026, with Howard Hughes Communities selling 307.7 residential acres at an average price of $1.2 million per acre and 9.8 commercial acres at an average price of $0.9 million per acre.
–Total Operating Assets NOI, including contributions from unconsolidated ventures, increased by $5.1 million, or 8% to a total of $73.0 million in the quarter compared to $67.9 million in the prior-year period.

–Announced intention to transition real estate model to an asset-light real estate development and asset management platform by monetizing select real estate assets through asset sales and joint ventures.

Net Operating Income (NOI)

We define NOI as operating revenues (rental income, tenant recoveries, and other revenue) less operating expenses (real estate taxes, repairs and maintenance, marketing, and other property expenses). NOI excludes straight-line rents and amortization of tenant incentives, net; interest expense, net; ground rent amortization; demolition costs; other income (loss); depreciation and amortization; development-related marketing costs; gain on sale or disposal of real estate and other assets, net; loss on extinguishment of debt; provision for impairment; and equity in earnings from unconsolidated ventures. This amount is presented as Operating Assets NOI throughout this document. Total Operating Assets NOI represents NOI as defined above with the addition of our share of NOI from unconsolidated ventures.

We believe that NOI is a useful supplemental measure of the performance of our Operating Assets segment because it provides a performance measure that reflects the revenues and expenses directly associated with owning and operating real estate properties. We use NOI to evaluate our operating performance on a property-by-property basis because NOI allows us to evaluate the impact that property-specific factors such as rental and occupancy rates, tenant mix, and operating costs have on our operating results, gross margins, and investment returns.
